Semina

A spoken-word track about the time zoo: rooms, tides, moons, a cat named Midnight. The words set the picture. Every line calls up footage of what it names: the moon from Méliès and Apollo, the Morse key from an Army Signal Corps reel, the trapeze from a 1901 circus, the ghost from a 1901 Scrooge. The beat does the cutting, the glitching and the color: fast runs of cuts resolve into held images that pulse on the beat.

How it was made Process

  1. Words first. The lyrics were timed word by word against the vocal, so each word lands on screen as it's spoken, with the newest word glowing.
  2. Images answer the words. Each line has themes (moon, ocean, circus, Morse, ghost, cat, grave, stars) and draws its footage from films that show them. Every clip was checked by eye; intertitles and captions were thrown out.
  3. Chop and hold. The edit is planned bar by bar. Runs of fast cuts on the drums (footage stuttered, reversed, retriggered on hits) resolve into held images that loop a single beat and punch in on every beat, then break back into cuts. Holds land where a line names something you can see, so the image has time to sit with the word, and hard snares stab them with flash-cuts of related footage.
  4. Glitch & color. Real codec datamoshing (deleted I-frames, repeated P-frames), stale-block moshing, gradient-map color bursts on kicks, macroblock corruption on snares, and strobes on hi-hats. The words glitch and strobe along with the picture.
